Mid-November to May Exam Master Schedule

An 18-Month Month-by-Month Roadmap Stretching Right Through to the Year 6 Exam Window

📅 Month-by-Month Roadmap (Mid-Nov Year 4 through May Year 6)

Stretching from an early mid-November launch straight through to the scholarship and selective testing window in May of Year 6.

Nov–Dec (Year 4): Launch & Core Vocab Phase 1: Foundation

  • Reading: Introduce diverse texts (fiction, biographies, editorials); focus on structural dissection.
  • Vocabulary: Establish root words, suffixes, and 10 new Tier-2 words weekly.
  • Math & Writing: Mental math fluency, basic fractions/decimals, 3-part narrative skeletons.

Jan–Feb (Year 5): Math & Skeletons Phase 1: Foundation

  • Numeracy: Ratio, proportion, and percentage calculations (calculator-free).
  • Writing: Practicing descriptive openings and compelling hooks.
  • Reasoning: Introduction to basic numerical series and shape pattern sequences.

Mar–Apr (Year 5): Geometry & Inference Phase 1: Foundation

  • Reading: Poetry and figurative language (similes, metaphors, imagery).
  • Numeracy: Perimeter, area, and composite shape problems.
  • Reasoning: Matrix puzzles and grid-based logical arrangements.

May–Jun (Year 5): Baseline & Logic Phase 2: Transition

  • Assessment: Sit an untimed baseline diagnostic paper to map initial strengths and gaps.
  • Thinking Skills: Formal logic puzzles, truth-teller/liar problems, and argument assumptions.
  • Numeracy: Multi-step word problem decomposition.

Jul–Aug (Year 5): Spatial & Writing Depth Phase 2: Transition

  • Thinking Skills: Spatial rotations, cube nets, and mirror/reflection patterns.
  • Writing: Persuasive and discursive essay structuring.
  • Review: Mid-point mini-mock test under loose time limits.

Sep–Oct (Year 5): ACER Writing Stamina Phase 2: Transition

  • ACER Focus: Introduce dual-writing bursts (2 back-to-back 25-minute tasks: creative + persuasive).
  • Numeracy: Speed arithmetic drills (averaging 1 minute per question).
  • Literacy: Humanities extraction and distractor elimination drills.

Nov–Dec (Year 5): Digital Selective Shift Phase 3: Simulation

  • Selective Focus: Shift reading comprehension and math reasoning practice to computer screens.
  • Thinking Skills: Advanced logical deduction and classification puzzles.
  • Review: Targeted error analysis on recurring math mistakes.

Jan–Feb (Year 6): First Full Simulations Phase 3: Simulation

  • Mock Test: Sit the first full-length, timed mock exam across all disciplines.
  • Error Logging: Deep-dive error analysis session over the weekend to review every missed question.
  • Focus: Time management and pacing adjustments.

Mar–Apr (Year 6): Fortnightly Mocks Phase 3: Simulation

  • Simulation: Run fortnightly full-length simulated exam blocks under strict test conditions.
  • Strategy: Refine guessing strategies for difficult multiple-choice questions ("don't get stuck").
  • Remediation: Isolate any weak sub-topics uncovered during mocks.

May (Year 6): Exam Window & Taper Peak & Taper

  • Tapering: Cut test volume by 50% to prevent burnout in the final weeks.
  • Maintenance: Light vocabulary reviews, mental math warm-ups, and essay structure checks.
  • Exam Execution: Sit ACER scholarship tests and the NSW Selective Placement Test with total confidence!

⚖️ Maintaining a Sustainable Weekly Rhythm

Because this is an extended 18-month runway, consistency easily trumps intensity. Keep weekly workloads light and manageable:

Weeknight Sessions (Mon–Thu)

30 to 45 minutes per night. Rotate topics across the week (e.g., Monday reading, Tuesday math drills, Wednesday logic puzzles, Thursday writing outline) so study never feels overwhelming.

Weekend Routine

1.5 to 2 hours total. Dedicate Saturday morning to a single timed sectional test or practice block, followed by a collaborative review session to talk through mistakes constructively.

Rest & Play

Protect downtime. Ensure your daughter maintains her normal extracurriculars, sports, and reading for pleasure. A balanced, happy child performs significantly better under exam conditions.
